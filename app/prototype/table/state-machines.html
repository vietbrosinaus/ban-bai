The scopes

You named three: table, card, pile. There are more, and two of the missing ones (your hand, and the selection) are where most of the confusion in the current app comes from. Cards and piles are the same thing, which is the single biggest simplification available.

The one collapse worth making

A card is a pile of one. If you build only piles, then "put a card on a pile", "put a pile on a pile" and "put a card on a card" are one operation instead of three, and every menu you write gets shorter.

State machines

Click any box to see only what enters and leaves it. Gold words are the input you give.

AA piece on the table (card or pile)

Everything on the felt lives this one life. Note that the interesting actions are self-loops on Resting: they change the piece without moving it anywhere.

BOne card, and who can see it

This is the machine that decides what has to be secret over the network. Peeking is the awkward one: it is a state that exists only for you, only while you hold the button.

CYour hand

Your hand is just a private pile with an owner. Everything that goes in came from the felt or from a person, and everything that leaves goes back to one of those.

DThe table

EThe session (this is the part P2P changes)

FThe pointer. This is where "intuitive" is decided

Every other machine describes the world. This one describes your hand on the mouse. If this machine is simple and has no ambiguous transitions, the app feels intuitive. If two transitions fire on the same input, it feels buggy. The current app mixes HTML drag-and-drop with pointer events, which is exactly that kind of ambiguity.

Not drawn, because they never leave Idle: double click flips the top card, wheel rotates, middle click takes one card. Those three are the whole reason the table feels direct.

The four ideas doing the work

What stays in the menu, and why

A thing earns a menu slot if it has no honest physical gesture, or if getting it wrong by accident would be annoying. Shuffle has a lovely gesture (shake the pile) but you must never shuffle by accident, so it keeps a menu slot too.

Try the model

Not the real UI. This drives the state machines above with buttons so you can check the model holds together. Click a pile to select it, then act on it. State is in memory, reload to reset.
